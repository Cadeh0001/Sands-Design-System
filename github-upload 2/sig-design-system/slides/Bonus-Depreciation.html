Tax Considerations

Bonus Depreciation — an Option on the Replacement

Something the replacement property can offer if you want it — not a reason to buy. Illustrative only; all figures and eligibility sit with your CPA.

01
Why it may be new to you
The tax code lets owners deduct a building's cost a little each year — apartments take 27.5 years. Because you inherited these buildings, that clock restarted at their value at the time, and the faster write-offs described here were likely never used.
02
What it does on a new NNN
An engineer studies the new property and identifies the parts that wear out faster — equipment, fixtures, paving, landscaping. Those parts can be written off over 5 to 15 years instead of decades, and a rule called bonus depreciation can speed that up further.
03
Where it shows up — and where it doesn't
It only works when you own the building itself — especially a newer one. If you own just the land and the tenant owns the building (a ground lease), there is little to write off.
What It Means for You
In the first years of owning the new property, these write-offs can cancel out some or all of the rent on your tax return. The rent still arrives in full — you may simply owe less tax on it for a while. How much depends on your situation; your CPA runs the actual numbers before anything is decided.
